Tempus Sales Copilot  /  case study, five slides
01 The problem

431 oncologists in Chicago. Which ten do you call on Monday?

A rep prepares a fifteen minute meeting with a chief medical officer by hand: Salesforce notes, a vendor market file, CMS volumes, recent publications, FDA labelling. Hours of assembly for one conversation, and the assembly is thrown away afterwards.

  • WhoWhich physicians see patients that Tempus's specific panels fit, rather than which ones simply see the most patients?
  • Why nowWhat changed this month that makes this call timely, and can I point at the dated source that changed?
  • What I sayAn opener and an objection answer that are tailored, specific, and true.

Everyone builds a generator. The hard part is the verifier.

Drafting a pitch is one model call. Knowing whether the pitch is true before a rep says a performance number to a chief medical officer, at a company operating under FDA labelling, is the part that has to be engineered. A confident wrong number costs more than a bland pitch.

Scope. Decision support for call preparation. Not a clinical recommendation, not a prediction of physician quality, and never spoken without a person reviewing it first.

02 The product

One screen: who to call, why now, and what to say.

The three outputs the brief asks for are not three features. They are one dossier a rep opens on the way into the building.

  • Ranked provider listOrdered by estimated eligible patient impact, not by headcount. Also rolled up to accounts, because a chief medical officer buys for a site: 60 physicians become 20 accounts, consolidating 19 UChicago oncologists into one conversation.
  • Why nowDated events that decay, each linking its own source. A physician's own December paper outranks a market wide FDA action.
  • The meeting scriptA thirty second opener assembled only from claims that cited evidence and survived every gate.
  • The objection handler"Turnaround time" answered with approximately nine days for xT, quoted from Tempus's FY2025 Form 10-K. Pricing has no approved source, so it is refused.

Verify before calling   NPPES says Internal Medicine, Medical Oncology. CMS says Hematology-Oncology. The conflict lowers confidence instead of being resolved silently behind the rep.

03 Ranking and explainability

A transparent weighted score, because there is no conversion history to learn from.

IngestMarket CSV, Markdown KB, CRM text. Each has its own parser; nothing about a physician is hard-coded.
→
EnrichNPPES, CMS Utilization, CMS Open Payments, ClinicalTrials.gov, PubMed.
→
ResolveNPI anchor, Jaro-Winkler 0.88 floor on names, cross-source consensus.
→
ScoreSix weighted signals, with a sensitivity pass on each.
  • 32% patient opportunity
  • 20% identity confidence
  • 17% panel fit
  • 13% CRM engagement
  • 10% record freshness
  • 8% market trial density
  • Panel fit scores match rate, indication fit multiplied by likelihood of testing, not volume. Patient count is already the opportunity term; multiplying it in twice would quietly turn the ranking into a headcount sort.
  • Identity confidence is a ranking input at 20%, so a provider we cannot resolve across sources cannot out-rank one we can. The cross-validator is load-bearing, not decorative.
  • Sensitivity analysis shows where a provider would rank if a signal were unavailable, so "why is this one third" has an answer rather than a bar chart.

Panel fit also names the panel. A colorectal-heavy practice routes to xT CDx on the KRAS companion diagnostic indication. A practice whose tissue is frequently inadequate routes to xF. A predominantly haematologic practice is marked limited fit, because xT CDx's labelled intended use is solid malignant neoplasms.

Why not a model? There is no real Tempus conversion history to train on. A learned ranker on simulated labels would manufacture false precision. Phase 2 adds a logistic model in shadow mode with explicit promotion criteria: 40 genuine outcomes, held-out precision@5, calibration review.

04 Prompts, gates and validation

Every generated sentence names its evidence and passes four gates.

// generation system prompt, abbreviated
You draft sales copy for a regulated medical
diagnostics company.
- You may ONLY assert what the supplied EVIDENCE states.
- Every claim must cite at least one evidence id.
- Never state a number that does not appear verbatim
  in the evidence you cite.
- If the evidence does not support a point, omit it.
  Do not hedge, do not approximate.
Respond ONLY with JSON:
{"claims":[{"text":"<one sentence>","evidenceIds":[...]}]}

The model never writes prose the user sees. It emits structured claims that each name their evidence, and the final copy is assembled from the claims that survive. You cannot check a paragraph. You can check a sentence that cites its sources.

// gate 4 auditor prompt, abbreviated
You are a strict evidence auditor for a regulated
medical diagnostics company.
Do not use outside knowledge. Do not be charitable.
{"verdict":"supported"|"partial"|"unsupported"}

Gemini 2.0 Flash drafts, Llama 3.3 70B on Groq audits, blind to the generation prompt. A model grading its own output is not a check.

22bugs the suite found in live data. Three of them:
  • Trial counts were page sizes. A 40-row page length was reported as the market total. Chicago actually has 751 recruiting cancer trials.
  • The territory was an alphabetical page. A limit=20 query returned 11 physicians whose surnames all began with A, and a single taxonomy filter excluded 172 of 430.
  • Gate 1 ran after the model call. A poisoned note was withheld from the rep, but had already reached the generator.
05 Assumptions and limits

What I assumed, and what I would not let it claim.

Assumptions I am making
  • The vendor CSV is a modelled estimate of annual oncology patients and tumour mix, not observed counts. It is treated as the least trusted source and cross-checked, because a stale panel file is what sends a rep to the wrong building.
  • Recent CRM contact indicates readiness, NPPES update recency indicates record quality, and cross-source agreement indicates the right physician was found.
  • The weights are product assumptions, not values learned from Tempus outcomes. Opportunity leads because patient impact is the brief's objective; identity and freshness are there to lower the cost of acting on a bad record.
  • Panel-fit rules, such as colorectal volume routing to xT CDx or inadequate tissue routing to xF, are call-planning heuristics, not clinical guidance.
  • A bounded 60 doctor working set is sufficient for a prototype. Market size and working set are always reported as separate numbers so a cost bound cannot be read as a territory size.

Every assumption above is visible in the interface, not buried in the code. An estimate is labelled an estimate, a contested field names the sources that disagree, and a number we do not have reads Unavailable rather than zero.

Limits I would not paper over
  • CMS utilization is annual, covers Original Medicare fee-for-service only, and suppresses counts below 11.
  • Market trial density is city-level and identical for every provider in a market. It carries 8% of the weight for that reason and is labelled as market-level in the interface.
  • ClinicalTrials.gov publishes no NPI for site staff, so a facility link is capped at 0.55 confidence and never presented as participation.
  • Pricing remains a declared gap. No approved source states it, so the objection handler refuses, pivots to the strongest supported evidence, and hands the rep a discovery question.
  • Missing information stays visible and lowers confidence rather than being invented, and every generated line still needs a person to read it before it is said aloud.
